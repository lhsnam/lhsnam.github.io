Turning complex biological results into figures people can read is part of my daily work. This is the short list I keep returning to, grouped by the decision it helps with.

## Where to look for ideas

- [Data Vis Dispatch](https://blog.datawrapper.de/category/data-vis-dispatch/) by Datawrapper. Highly recommended, a regular roundup of good charts from the news.
- [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/)
- [FlowingData](http://flowingdata.com)
- Newsroom graphics: The Economist, the Wall Street Journal and the Financial Times all publish very clean charts worth studying.

---

## Guides

Most of these guides come from Datawrapper's [data vis do's and don'ts](https://blog.datawrapper.de/category/datavis-dos-and-donts/). The links below are my own top picks.

### Choosing colours

1. [What to consider when choosing colours for data visualization](https://blog.datawrapper.de/colors/)
2. [Make grey your best friend](https://visualisingdata.com/2015/01/make-grey-best-friend/)
3. [An alternative to pink and blue: colors for gender data](https://blog.datawrapper.de/gendercolor/)
4. [How do your colourblind and colour-weak readers see your colours?](https://blog.datawrapper.de/colorblindness-part1/)
5. [What to consider when visualizing data for colourblind readers](https://blog.datawrapper.de/colorblindness-part2/)
6. Optional: [What's it like to be colourblind?](https://blog.datawrapper.de/colorblindness-part3/)
7. [How to pick more beautiful colours for your data visualizations](https://blog.datawrapper.de/beautifulcolors/)

### Text in charts

1. [What to consider when using text in data visualizations](https://blog.datawrapper.de/text-in-data-visualizations/)
2. [Which fonts to use for your charts and tables](https://blog.datawrapper.de/fonts-for-data-visualization/)

### Annotation: less, but better

1. [What questions to ask when creating charts](https://blog.datawrapper.de/better-charts/)
2. [Respect your readers' time](https://blog.datawrapper.de/readers-time/)

## Books and style guides

- *Storytelling with Data: A Data Visualization Guide for Business Professionals* by Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic. The book I would start with. [Author's site](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/).
- *Intelligence Data Styleguide* (v2.0), a practical style guide for presenting data consistently.
- *Visualising Knowledge* (2022), on turning knowledge into visual form.

## Maps

[Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) provides free vector and raster map data at 1:10m, 1:50m and 1:110m scales.
